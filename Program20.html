Core Concepts

Machine learning is a branch of artificial intelligence that uses data and algorithms to let computers learn and make predictions without being explicitly programmed for every task. Data: Information collected and prepared to train the computer program.Model: A mathematical representation that learns to recognize patterns from training data.Training: The process of adjusting a model's settings so it makes accurate guesses on new data. [1] (https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter/machine-learning-explained), [2] (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fa_V9fP2tpU), [3] (https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/machine-learning)Main TypesSupervised learning: The model trains on labeled data with known correct answers, such as sorting emails into spam or safe.Unsupervised learning: The model looks at unlabeled data on its own to find hidden structures or groups.Reinforcement learning: An agent learns to reach a goal through trial and error, receiving rewards or penalties.
